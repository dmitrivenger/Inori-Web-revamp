What is a DPN

A digital record of a payment that's been approved

A Digital Payment Note (DPN) is created the moment a buyer approves a supplier's invoice. It holds the approved payment in a single record that the buyer, the supplier and INORI all see.

Every DPN has a serial number, the legal names and registration numbers of the buyer and supplier, the amount in shekels, when it was issued, the due date, who owns it right now and what state it's in.

The life of a DPN

  1. Awaiting buyer approval
  2. Issuance pending
  3. Active
  4. Pending settlement
  5. Settled

If there's a dispute, the DPN can be marked as disputed or frozen, and nothing moves forward until it's resolved.

How it works

Reverse factoring in four steps

The buyer approves the invoice

The buyer confirms who the supplier is, the amount and the due date. NEVO records all of it in a Digital Payment Note (DPN) and issues it to the supplier.

The supplier decides

Keep the DPN until the due date, or ask INORI for early payment. An approved invoice doesn't automatically mean approved funding.

INORI reviews and pays

INORI looks at the request. If it's approved and the supplier accepts the terms, INORI pays by bank transfer, checks the bank evidence, and the DPN is transferred to INORI.

The buyer pays on the due date

On the original due date, the buyer pays whoever owns the DPN at that point. Once the payment is matched, the DPN is marked as settled.

Two ways to use NEVO

Led by the buyer, or started by the supplier

Reverse factoring (buyer-led)

The buyer approves the invoice. The supplier can ask for early payment, and on the due date the buyer pays whoever owns the DPN.

Factoring (supplier-led)

A supplier can also ask for funding against an eligible, active DPN it owns. The other party has to agree, and INORI reviews the request before any money is sent.

One record for both sides

Approvals, ownership, funding steps and proof of payment all stay together, from the first approval to the final payment.

Technology and controls

The ledger tracks ownership. The money moves through banks.

SORA Nexus, built on Hyperledger Iroha v3, records every change in a DPN's state and ownership. INORI's payments and the buyer's payment go through regular bank transfers outside the blockchain, and the proof is then recorded and matched in NEVO.

  • A DPN moves to INORI only after INORI has checked the bank evidence and signed that specific transfer.
  • A DPN is marked as settled only after the payment proof is matched and INORI approves it.
  • Signing keys are created, encrypted and used on the customer's own device. No outside custodian holds them.
  • Only verified, authorised company users can take actions on the platform.

FAQ

Questions we hear often

What is reverse factoring?

It starts with the buyer. The buyer approves the invoice, the supplier can ask for early payment, and on the due date the buyer pays whoever owns the payment at that point. In NEVO, that obligation and its ownership are recorded as a DPN.

Does the buyer have to pay earlier?

No. The buyer pays on the original due date. If the DPN has been transferred to INORI, the buyer simply pays INORI instead of the supplier, under the terms of the transaction.

Does the supplier have to take early payment?

Not at all. The supplier can ask for funding or just wait for the due date. Any funding depends on eligibility, review, approval and the agreed terms.

Is every approved invoice funded?

No. An approved invoice is not the same as approved funding. INORI reviews every company and every invoice, and the supplier sees the terms before deciding whether to go ahead.

What does the blockchain actually do?

SORA Nexus, built on Hyperledger Iroha v3, is the ledger where each DPN's state and ownership are recorded. The money itself, INORI's funding and the buyer's payment, moves through regular bank transfers. The proof is then recorded and matched in NEVO.

Who can join NEVO?

Right now, registration is open to Israeli limited companies (Ltd) only. You'll need your company registration and bank details, the work e-mail of an authorised representative, and the company, ownership and ID documents we ask for.

Can a supplier ask for factoring directly?

Yes, if it owns an eligible, active DPN. The company needs to be verified, the other party has to agree, and INORI reviews the request before sending any money.

What if an invoice is paid late or disputed?

That's covered by the agreements for that specific transaction, including who repays the advance and what happens while the dispute is sorted out. Go over these terms with INORI before you accept funding.

Who can see my company and invoice details?

Only authorised users have access in NEVO. If you have questions about how your information is handled, get in touch with INORI.
