The problem

Suppliers wait months to get paid.

Long payment terms tie up working capital across the supply chain. Invoices sit in queues, financing is slow and paper-heavy, and small suppliers carry the cost of waiting.

The solution

One digital note. Liquidity on day one.

With DPN, the payer issues a digital payment note for an approved invoice. The supplier can hold it to maturity or sell it to a funder for early payment — with every step recorded on a tamper-proof ledger.

How it works

From invoice to early payment in four steps

The payer issues a DPN

An approved supplier payment is issued as a digital payment note and recorded on the blockchain ledger.

The supplier receives it

The note is transferred to the supplier with a clear amount, maturity date and full audit trail.

Early payment from a funder

The supplier can sell the note to a bank or factoring company and receive cash immediately. The change of ownership is recorded on the ledger.

Settlement at maturity

On the due date the payer pays the current holder of the note. The full history stays verifiable.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a Digital Payment Note (DPN)?

A DPN is a digital record of a payer's commitment to pay a supplier a set amount on a set date. It is issued and tracked on a blockchain ledger, so ownership and history are always verifiable.

How does a supplier get paid early?

Once the supplier holds a DPN, it can sell the note to a participating funder and receive payment before the due date. At maturity, the payer pays the funder.

Does the payer have to change its payment terms?

No. The payer keeps its existing terms and pays on the original due date — to whoever holds the note at that time.
